Lacing for long descents

Toes hitting the front of the boot on the way down? Three lacing changes that help, and when to use each one.

Most foot trouble on a long day starts on the way down. The foot slides forward, the toes meet the front of the boot, and a small rub becomes a blister by the valley.

Lock the heel

Use the top eyelets to make a heel lock: pass each lace under the opposite loop before you tie. The ankle stays back in the heel cup on steep ground.

Two zones

Tie a surgeon’s knot at the instep. Keep the forefoot relaxed for swelling feet, and the upper laces firm for support.

Check again at the top

Feet swell over the day. Stop at the summit or the col and relace before you start down. It takes a minute and saves an evening.
